# Force Majeure in Commercial Leases: Case Summaries

*Fake case-law digest for demo purposes only. Not real legal citations.*

## Case 1: Meridian Retail Partners v. Harborview Properties LLC (Fictional, 2021)

Meridian, a tenant operating a chain of retail stores, invoked the force majeure clause in its
commercial lease after a regional supply-chain disruption prevented store deliveries for eleven
weeks. The lease's force majeure clause listed "acts of God, war, and governmental action" but did
not explicitly mention supply-chain disruption. The court held that a force majeure clause must be
read narrowly against the party invoking it, and that a foreseeable commercial risk (supply-chain
delay) does not qualify unless the clause uses broad catch-all language such as "or any other cause
beyond the reasonable control of the party."

**Key holding:** Absent broad catch-all language, force majeure clauses are construed narrowly and
do not automatically extend to ordinary commercial risks.

## Case 2: Bellwood Café Group v. Falcon Crest Holdings (Fictional, 2022)

Bellwood sought rent abatement during a mandated closure order tied to a public-health emergency.
The lease's force majeure clause included "governmental order or regulation." The court found this
squarely within the clause's plain language and enforced a proportional rent abatement for the
closure period, but rejected Bellwood's claim for lost profits, holding that force majeure clauses
excuse performance (e.g., paying full rent for full access) rather than acting as a general
insurance policy against lost business.

**Key holding:** A force majeure clause excuses the specific performance obstructed by the covered
event; it does not create an independent damages remedy.

## Case 3: Northgate Logistics v. Union Terminal Properties (Fictional, 2023)

Northgate argued that a prolonged utility outage at its leased warehouse constituted force majeure.
The lease required the invoking party to provide written notice "within 10 business days of the
triggering event." Northgate provided notice on day 14. The court held that strict compliance with
notice provisions is a condition precedent to relief under a force majeure clause, and enforced the
clause's notice deadline even though the underlying event (utility outage) would otherwise have
qualified.

**Key holding:** Notice deadlines in force majeure clauses are typically enforced as conditions
precedent; late notice can forfeit an otherwise valid claim.
