## Purpose

This memo summarizes standard indemnification clause structures used in commercial supply and
services contracts, for internal reference when reviewing vendor agreements.

## Standard Structure

A typical indemnification clause has four components:

1. **Trigger** -- the type of claim or loss covered (e.g., third-party claims, IP infringement,
   breach of confidentiality).
2. **Scope** -- whether indemnification covers direct damages only, or extends to consequential,
   incidental, and punitive damages.
3. **Procedure** -- notice requirements, control of defense, and settlement consent rights.
4. **Cap and Carve-Outs** -- liability caps (often tied to fees paid in the prior 12 months) and
   carve-outs from the cap for gross negligence, willful misconduct, or IP infringement.

## Common Negotiation Points

- **Mutual vs. one-sided indemnification.** Vendors often propose one-sided clauses favoring
  themselves; customers typically negotiate for mutual indemnification covering both parties'
  breaches.
- **Duty to defend vs. duty to indemnify.** A "duty to defend" obligates the indemnifying party to
  pay defense costs as they are incurred, not just reimburse a final judgment. This distinction
  matters significantly for cash-flow exposure during litigation.
- **Basket and cap interaction.** Some contracts set a minimum threshold ("basket") before
  indemnification obligations apply, in addition to the overall liability cap.

## Recommendation

For vendor contracts involving data processing, we recommend: (1) mutual indemnification, (2) an
uncapped carve-out for data breach and confidentiality violations, and (3) a duty-to-defend
obligation rather than reimbursement-only language.
