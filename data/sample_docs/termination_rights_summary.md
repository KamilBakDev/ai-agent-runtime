# Summary: Termination Rights in Commercial Leases

## Termination for Convenience

Some commercial leases grant the tenant a limited right to terminate early ("termination for
convenience") in exchange for a termination fee, typically calculated as a multiple of monthly
rent or the unamortized portion of landlord concessions (tenant improvement allowances, broker
commissions). These clauses usually require advance written notice, commonly 90 to 180 days.

## Termination for Cause

Termination for cause allows either party to end the lease upon an uncured material breach.
Typical structure:

1. Non-breaching party delivers written notice describing the breach.
2. Breaching party has a cure period (often 10 days for monetary breaches, 30 days for
   non-monetary breaches).
3. If uncured, the non-breaching party may terminate and pursue damages.

## Termination Tied to Force Majeure

Many leases include a long-stop termination right tied to extended force majeure events: if a
force majeure event (see companion case summaries) prevents performance for longer than a
specified period (commonly 90 to 180 continuous days), either party may terminate without
penalty. This is distinct from the shorter-term rent abatement relief typically available during
the force majeure event itself.

## Practical Note

When drafting or reviewing termination clauses, confirm whether the force majeure clause's notice
requirements (see case summaries) also govern the long-stop termination right, since some leases
apply a separate, stricter notice standard to termination elections.
